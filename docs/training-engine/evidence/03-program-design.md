# 03 – Program Design: Evidence Base for the Training Engine

Scope: healthy adults 18+, general fitness, home or gym. Compiled 2026-10-13.
Grades: **A** = position stand / guideline; **B** = systematic review / meta-analysis; **C** = practitioner consensus, narrative review, or engine-design inference.
Citation keys in [brackets] refer to the Sources list at the end.

**Headline anchor:** The 2026 ACSM resistance-training position stand [ACSM-2026] is the newest top-level source. It is an overview of 137 systematic reviews. Its main message is that the most important thing is doing any resistance training regularly. Most secondary variables (equipment, failure, periodization model, rest length) had no consistent effect on outcomes for average healthy adults. The engine should therefore fix a small set of hard rules and leave other variables to preference and adherence.

## Topic 1 – Mapping goals to program variables

### Key conclusions
- **Universal floor.** Train all major muscle groups at least 2 sessions/week with high effort, and do at least 2 sets per exercise. **A** [ACSM-2026]. WHO adds 150–300 min/week of moderate aerobic activity (or 75–150 min vigorous) and muscle strengthening on 2+ days/week. **A** [WHO-2020]
- **Strength goal.** Use heavy loads (about ≥80% 1RM) for 2–3 sets per exercise. Gains flatten at about 2–3 sets per exercise. **A** [ACSM-2026]
- **Muscle-gain goal.** Hypertrophy follows weekly volume: at least 10 sets per muscle group per week, with diminishing returns around 18–20 sets. Load matters less than volume when sets are taken close to failure. Stopping 1–2 reps short of failure gives similar results. **A** [ACSM-2026; NFPT summary]. The older meta-analysis [Schoenfeld-2016] found 2×/week per muscle beat 1×/week (ES 0.49 vs 0.30). The 2026 stand found frequency does not matter once volume is equated. **B/A**
- **Power.** Use 30–70% 1RM moved as fast as possible, with low volume (reps × sets ≤24). **A** [ACSM-2026]. This is a minor goal for this app.
- **General health / "just start moving".** Any resistance training gives the largest relative gains compared with none. Minimal doses produce real strength and function gains. **A** [ACSM-2026]. In cohort data, muscle strengthening is linked to about 10–20% lower all-cause, cardiovascular and cancer mortality. The benefit peaks at about 30–60 min/week, and it is unclear whether more adds anything (J-shaped curve). **B** [Momma-2022]. For beginners, ACSM 2011 suggests 1 set of 10–15 reps at 40–70% 1RM. **A** [Garber-2011]
- **Fat loss.** Exercise alone gives modest weight loss (−1.5 to −3.5 kg) and fat loss (−1.3 to −2.6 kg). Aerobic training and HIIT do not differ when energy expenditure is matched. Resistance training protects lean mass during weight loss (about +0.8 kg). **B** [Bellicha-2021]. Resistance training alone reduces body fat by about 1.46 percentage points and fat mass by about 0.55 kg, and lowers visceral fat (SMD −0.49). **B** [Wewege-2022]. Combining resistance training with calorie restriction works best for body fat % (about −3.8%). **B** [Lopez-2022]. Clinically meaningful loss needs more than 250 min/week of activity. 150–250 min/week prevents weight gain. **A** (2009, older) [Donnelly-2009]
- **Mobility/flexibility.** Stretch at least 2–3 days/week (daily is better). Hold 10–30 s (30–60 s for older adults), for a total of about 60 s per muscle group. **A** [Garber-2011]. Full-range resistance training improves range of motion about as much as stretching does (ES −0.22, n.s.). **B** [Afonso-2021]

### Engine implications – goal parameter table
| Goal | RT sessions/wk | Sets/muscle/wk | Load / reps | RIR target | Aerobic share of total weekly time | Mobility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Just start moving | 2 (min) | 2–4 | 10–15 reps, light–moderate | 3–4 | 40–60% (walking-based) | 5 min/session optional |
| General health | 2–3 | 6–10 | 8–15 reps | 2–3 | ~50% (toward 150 min/wk) | 2–3×/wk |
| Fat loss | 2–4 | 8–12 | 8–15 reps | 1–3 | 50–60% (toward 200–300 min/wk incl. walking) | optional |
| Muscle gain | 3–5 | 10–20 | 6–15 reps (5–30 acceptable) | 0–3 | 10–20% (keep it to retain fitness) | optional |
| Strength | 3–4 | 6–12 on main lifts | main lifts 3–6 reps at ≥80% 1RM, 2–3 sets; accessories 8–12 | 1–3 | 10–20% | optional |
| Mobility/flexibility | 2 (full-ROM RT) | 4–8 | 8–15, full ROM, slow eccentric | 2–3 | 20–30% | 5+ days/wk, ≥5 min/wk per region, 60 s per muscle per session |

- Hard floors (never violated): every major muscle group trained ≥2×/week once the user has ≥2 days, and ≥2 sets per exercise, except in 10-min sessions (see Topic 3). **A**
- Cap weekly sets per muscle at 20 by default. Start beginners at the low end and add 1–2 sets per muscle per week only when sessions are completed. **C**

### Uncertainty
- The volume ceiling for hypertrophy (around 20 sets) is a point of diminishing returns, not a proven maximum.
- Beginners respond to almost any program, so goal differences matter mostly after 3–6 months.
- The Momma J-curve comes from observational data with self-reported activity. Do not cap strength training because of it.

## Topic 2 – Weekly structure by available days

### Key conclusions
- When weekly volume is equal, split routines and full-body routines give the same results. A meta-analysis of 14 studies (n=392) found no difference in bench or lower-limb strength, elbow flexor/extensor CSA, vastus lateralis size, or lean mass (all p>0.29). **B** [Ramos-Campo-2024]
- Hypertrophy does not differ between 1 day/week and more than 5 days/week when volume is equal. Strength may benefit slightly from more frequency, but the effect fades when volume is equal. **A** [ACSM-2026]
- Early trial: in trained men, total-body 3×/week and a 1×/week-per-muscle split gave similar 1RM. Total-body gave greater forearm-flexor thickness. **B-level RCT** [Schoenfeld-2015]
- Per-session volume has diminishing returns. Beyond about 2 direct sets per muscle per session for strength, or about 11 fractional sets per session for hypertrophy, extra sets show no detectable benefit. This argues for spreading volume across days rather than putting it all in one session. **B (preprint)** [Remmert-2025]
- NSCA practice: total-body, upper/lower, and body-part splits are all valid. Choose based on goal, available days, and preference. **C** [NSCA-Hoffman]

### Engine implications – default templates (beginner / general)
Split choice is a scheduling tool, not an outcome driver. Choose the split that reaches ≥2×/week per muscle and keeps per-session sets per muscle at ≤6–8.

| Days/wk | Default | Structure | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Full-body | FB (2–3 sets per pattern) | Below the ACSM floor. Show a prompt to add snacks or a walk (Topic 3). Volume-equated 1×/wk still works for hypertrophy. |
| 2 | Full-body A/B | A: squat, horizontal push, horizontal pull, hinge-accessory, core. B: hinge, vertical push, vertical pull, lunge/single-leg, carry/core | Meets ≥2×/wk per muscle. Best minimal plan. |
| 3 | Full-body A/B/A, B/A/B | Same A/B, alternating weeks | Classic beginner default. Space sessions ≥48 h apart per muscle [Garber-2011]. |
| 4 | Upper/Lower ×2 (U1/L1/U2/L2) | U: H-push, H-pull, V-push, V-pull, arms. L: squat, hinge, single-leg, calf/core | For general fitness, FB A/B ×2 is an equal option. |
| 5 | U/L/U/L + FB (or + conditioning/mobility day) | — | For fat loss or health: 3–4 RT days plus 1–2 aerobic days. |
| 6 | Push/Pull/Legs ×2, or U/L ×3 | — | Only for intermediate+ users wanting muscle gain. Otherwise 4 RT days + 2 aerobic/mobility days. |

Rules:
- **Coverage rule:** each week, every pattern (squat, hinge, horizontal push, horizontal pull, vertical push, vertical pull, single-leg, core/carry) appears at least once. Squat, hinge, push and pull families each appear ≥2× if days ≥2. **C**
- **Recovery rule:** do not train the same primary pattern heavily on consecutive days. If the user picks consecutive days with FB, alternate heavy and light. **C** (ACSM 48 h is grade A for muscle groups.)
- **Missed-day rule:** because results depend on weekly volume, not frequency, a missed session's sets may be moved to the remaining sessions, up to the per-session cap. **B/C**

### Uncertainty
- Most split studies last 8–12 weeks and use young men. Evidence for 1 day/week at equal volume is thin, and in practice the volume often is not reached.
- The Remmert per-session values are a preprint, so treat them as provisional.

## Topic 3 – Single-session structure and time allocation

### Key conclusions
- **Warm-up helps in general.** In a review of 32 high-quality studies, warm-up improved performance in 79% of measures. There was little evidence of harm. **B** [Fradkin-2010]. Active warm-up raises muscle temperature, speeds oxygen kinetics, and can cause potentiation. The effects fade if there is a long delay before the work. **C** [McGowan-2015]
- **Resistance-training warm-up specifics.** Evidence on adding a general warm-up before the specific one is mixed. Specific warm-up sets (including heavier ones) help maximal strength and rep performance most. **B (small SR, 11 studies)** [Ribeiro-2021]. For time-efficiency, limit the warm-up to exercise-specific warm-up sets. **C** [Iversen-2021]
- **2026 Delphi consensus (athletes).** Use general (aerobic) work plus specific work. Prefer dynamic stretching over static. A short warm-up is ≤5 min. Re-warm-ups should be shorter than the first one. **C (expert consensus)** [Boullosa-2026]
- **Static stretching before lifting.** Static stretching right before activity lowers performance by about 3.7% on average. Holds of ≥60 s per muscle cause larger losses (about −4.6%) than shorter holds (about −1.1%). Dynamic stretching improves performance by about 1.3%. Following stretching with dynamic activity removes most of the loss. No clear effect on injury. **B** [Behm-2016]
- **Chronic stretching** gives small strength (d=0.30) and hypertrophy (d=0.20) gains. These are not a substitute for lifting at normal doses. **B** [Warneke-2024]
- **Time-efficient lifting.** Prioritize bilateral multi-joint lifts (≥1 leg press/squat type, 1 upper pull, 1 upper push). Do at least 4 sets per muscle group per week, in the 6–15 RM range. Rest 1–2 min for untrained users and ≥2 min for trained users. Supersets, drop sets, and rest-pause roughly halve session time. Frequent sessions of about 15 min are a valid option. Stretch only if flexibility is a goal. **C (narrative review by leading researchers)** [Iversen-2021]. Short rest (<1 min) vs long rest (>1 min) did not change strength outcomes. **A** [ACSM-2026]
- **Exercise snacks.** A meta-analysis of 11 RCTs (n=414, inactive adults) used bouts of ≤5 min, done ≥2 times a day, 3–7 days/week, for 4–12 weeks. Snacks improved cardiorespiratory fitness (and muscular endurance in older adults). They did not clearly change strength, body fat, blood pressure, or lipids. More than 90% of sessions were completed. **B** [Rodriguez-2026]

### Engine implications – session slot lists
Slot codes: **WU** warm-up; **M1/M2** main compound (strength priority); **A** accessory; **SS** superset pair (opposing patterns); **C** conditioning; **CD** cool-down/mobility. Pattern-family names follow Topic 4.

| Duration | Slots (in order) | Pattern content |
|---|---|---|
| **10 min** | WU 1–2 min (dynamic moves, ramp-up reps of 1st exercise) → circuit/SS 7–8 min → CD 0–1 min | 2 supersets: (lower-body squat or hinge + upper push), (single-leg or hinge + upper pull). 2–3 rounds. Count as ≥1 set per pattern. Alternatively an "exercise snack" (≤5 min) ×2/day. |
| **20 min** | WU 3 min → SS1 (M1 lower + M2 upper push) 7 min → SS2 (lower #2 + upper pull) 7 min → core/carry 2 min → CD 1 min | 2–3 sets each. Rotate squat/hinge and H/V push-pull between sessions A/B. |
| **30 min** | WU 4–5 min → M1 lower compound 7 min → SS (upper push + upper pull) 9 min → A: single-leg or hinge accessory + core 7 min → CD 2 min | 3 sets main, 2–3 sets accessory. Conditioning only on goal = fat loss (replace accessory with 6–8 min intervals). |
| **45 min** | WU 5–6 min → M1 7–9 min → M2 7–9 min → SS (push/pull or second lower) 8–10 min → A (arms/calves/core) 6 min → C 0–8 min (goal-dependent) → CD 2–3 min | Fat loss/health: C = 8–10 min. Muscle gain: replace C with extra accessory sets. |
| **60 min** | WU 6–8 min → M1 10 min → M2 10 min → M3/SS 10 min → A1/A2 10–12 min → C 0–12 min → CD 3–5 min | Strength: longer rest (2–3 min) on M1/M2. Mobility goal: CD 8–10 min of static stretching (after lifting). |
| **90 min** | WU 8–10 min → M1–M3 30 min → accessories 25 min → C 15–20 min (after lifting) → CD 5–10 min | Only for intermediate+ users or combined sessions. Above about 11 hard sets per muscle per session, extra work adds little, so add aerobic or mobility instead. |

Warm-up construction rules:
1. 1–3 min of general aerobic work only if the session is ≥30 min, or if the environment/user is cold. **C**
2. Dynamic mobility for the session's patterns (30–90 s). **B** [Behm-2016]
3. Specific ramp-up sets before M1: for loaded lifts, about 2 sets (e.g., ~50% × 8, ~75% × 3 of working load). Before later compounds, 0–1 ramp-up set. **B/C** [Ribeiro-2021; Iversen-2021]
4. No static holds ≥60 s per muscle before lifting. Short holds (<30 s) are allowed when followed by dynamic work. **B** [Behm-2016]
5. Static stretching and long mobility work go in CD or on separate days. **C**

### Uncertainty
- Warm-up RCTs mostly use athletes and performance tests, not long-term training outcomes. Whether skipping a warm-up raises injury risk in recreational lifters is unknown.
- The time allocations above are engine design choices (**C**) built on graded principles. They are not tested protocols.

## Topic 4 – Exercise selection and order

### Key conclusions
- **Order affects strength, not hypertrophy.** Strength gains are greatest for exercises done early in the session. Doing multi-joint lifts first favours multi-joint strength (ES 0.32), and doing single-joint lifts first favours single-joint strength (ES −0.58). Order has no effect on hypertrophy (ES 0.03). **B** [Nunes-2021]. The ACSM 2026 stand confirms this. **A** [ACSM-2026]
- Order exercises by goal priority, not muscle size. Exercises done first get more reps and volume. Pre-exhaustion does not increase recruitment of the larger muscle. **B (narrative SR)** [Simao-2012]
- NSCA practice: power/complex lifts first, then multi-joint, then single-joint. Alternate opposing or upper/lower muscle groups to keep up intensity. Pairing unrelated exercises saves time. **C** [NSCA-Hoffman]
- **Unilateral vs bilateral.** Each improves its own trained task more (bilateral strength ES 0.56 for bilateral training; unilateral strength favours unilateral). Hypertrophy is similar. **B** [Kassiano-2025]. Unilateral training also builds strength in the untrained limb (cross-education). **A** [ACSM-2026]. A second meta-analysis favours unilateral for jumping, but its reported statistics have internal errors. **B, low confidence** [Zhang-2023]
- **Equipment.** Machines vs free weights do not differ for strength. Elastic bands improve strength and hypertrophy. Home-based training improves strength, endurance, and balance. **A** [ACSM-2026]. Bands give strength gains similar to conventional resistance for upper body (SMD −0.01) and lower body (SMD 0.09). **B** [Lopes-2019]. Bodyweight push-ups and pull-ups are well supported, but evidence for bodyweight-only lower-body training is scarce. Bands are suitable at home, but heavy lower-body multi-joint loading needs conventional equipment for strength goals. **C** [Iversen-2021]
- **Multi-joint vs single-joint.** Data are insufficient to rank these for hypertrophy. **A** [ACSM-2026]. Multi-joint exercises are preferred for time-efficiency because they train more muscle per set. **C** [Iversen-2021]
- **Push/pull balance.** No meta-analytic evidence for a specific ratio. Practitioner consensus is to match horizontal and vertical pulling volume to pushing volume at least 1:1 (many coaches use ≥1:1 pull:push for desk workers). **C (no primary source verified)**

### Engine implications
**Pattern families** (each exercise tagged with one primary family): `squat`, `hinge`, `single_leg` (lunge/split squat/step-up), `h_push`, `v_push`, `h_pull`, `v_pull`, `core_anti_ext/rot`, `carry`, `isolation_upper`, `isolation_lower`, `conditioning`.

**Ordering rules (deterministic sort key):**
1. Explosive/plyometric (if programmed) → 2. user-priority lift (goal = strength: the target lift) → 3. bilateral multi-joint lower → 4. multi-joint upper → 5. unilateral multi-joint → 6. isolation → 7. core/carry → 8. conditioning → 9. static stretching.
- Exception: for goal = muscle gain, a lagging muscle's isolation exercise may move up. Hypertrophy does not depend on order. **B**
- In supersets, pair non-competing families: lower+upper, or push+pull. Never pair two grip-heavy or two spinal-loading exercises (e.g., deadlift + bent row). **C**

**Coverage/balance rules:**
- Weekly pull sets ≥ push sets (ratio 1.0–1.5). Weekly hinge sets ≈ squat sets (ratio 0.7–1.3). **C**
- ≥1 unilateral lower exercise per week (cross-education, balance, home-friendly). **A/C**
- Exercise variety: keep the main lifts the same for 4–8 weeks so progress can be measured, and rotate accessories freely. **C**

**Equipment substitution:** the substitution graph should allow free weight ↔ machine ↔ band ↔ bodyweight within a pattern family with no penalty to expected outcomes for general goals. **A**. For goal = strength with lower-body bodyweight only, show a note that gains may plateau without load, and prefer single-leg progressions (split squat → rear-foot-elevated → pistol progressions), tempo, and higher reps taken near failure. **C**

### Uncertainty
- Most order studies used untrained people in short trials.
- Hypertrophy evidence for bands rests on one review. For bodyweight-only training, home-based RT is the closest evidence.

## Topic 5 – Concurrent training (aerobic + resistance)

### Key conclusions
- Updated meta-analysis (43 studies): adding aerobic training does **not** reduce maximal strength (SMD −0.06) or hypertrophy (SMD −0.01). It does blunt explosive strength (SMD −0.28). The power loss is larger when both are done in the same session, and was not significant when sessions were ≥3 h apart. Aerobic mode, frequency (<5 vs >5 sessions/week), training status, and age did not moderate the results. **B** [Schumann-2022]
- Earlier meta-analysis (21 studies): concurrent training reduced hypertrophy (ES 0.85 vs 1.23 for strength only) and power. Running interfered more than cycling. Higher aerobic frequency and duration correlated with greater interference (r up to −0.75). **B, older** [Wilson-2012]
- Order within a session: doing resistance first gave about 6.9% greater lower-body dynamic strength than aerobic first. Hypertrophy, static strength, VO2max, and body fat % were not affected by order. **B** [Eddens-2018]
- ACSM 2026 found insufficient data on same-session combined training. **A** [ACSM-2026]

### Engine implications
- Concurrent training is safe by default for all goals. Do not reduce aerobic work for muscle-gain or strength users unless aerobic volume is high. **B**
- **Same-session order:** resistance first, then conditioning, when goal ∈ {strength, muscle gain}. For goal = fat loss/health, either order is allowed; default to resistance first. If the user's primary goal is endurance, aerobic may go first. **B**
- **Separation:** if both are on the same day and goal ∈ {strength, power}, suggest ≥3 h apart when the user's schedule allows. **B**
- **Interference guards (C, from Wilson trends):** for strength or muscle-gain goals, keep hard aerobic work at ≤3 sessions and ≤~90 min/week by default. Prefer cycling, rowing, or walking over high-volume running. Place hard intervals away from leg-day (not within 24 h before a heavy lower session).

### Uncertainty
- Interference evidence is mostly short-term (<12 weeks). Effects in highly trained or high-volume endurance athletes may differ, but they are out of scope.
- The Wilson and Schumann results disagree on hypertrophy. Prefer the newer and larger Schumann analysis.

## Topic 6 – Flexibility/mobility dose

### Key conclusions
- ACSM: ≥2–3 days/week (daily is more effective). Hold 10–30 s to the point of mild tightness (30–60 s for older adults). Do 2–4 repetitions so each muscle gets a total of 60 s. PNF uses a 3–6 s contraction at 20–75% of max, then a 10–30 s assisted stretch. **A** [Garber-2011]
- Weekly total time drives ROM gains (≥5 min/week per muscle group). Time per session did not matter, and higher frequency helped. Static stretching outperformed ballistic stretching and PNF for long-term ROM. Suggested: ≥5 days/week. **B** [Thomas-2018]
- Resistance training through full range of motion improves ROM similarly to stretching. **B** [Afonso-2021]
- Static stretching is best placed after training or in separate sessions (see Topic 3). **B** [Behm-2016]

### Engine implications
- **Default (all goals):** CD includes 1 stretch per main muscle trained, 2 × 30 s. **A**
- **Goal = mobility:** a daily 5–10 min mobility block (5–7 days/week) giving each target region ≥60 s per session and ≥5 min/week. Static stretching is the main method, plus 2 days/week of full-ROM resistance work (e.g., deep split squats, RDLs, full-ROM rows/presses). **A/B**
- Mobility can be a standalone session type (10–20 min) on rest days. It does not count toward resistance-training volume. **C**

### Uncertainty
- Long-term ROM gains are partly increased stretch tolerance rather than tissue change. Whether that matters functionally for general users is debated.

## Cross-cutting disagreements
1. **Frequency for hypertrophy:** Schoenfeld 2016 (2×/wk better) vs ACSM 2026 / Ramos-Campo 2024 (no effect at equal volume). Engine: keep 2×/wk as a practical default because it limits per-session volume, but do not treat it as essential.
2. **Concurrent interference on hypertrophy:** Wilson 2012 (yes) vs Schumann 2022 (no). Engine follows Schumann and adds mild guards.
3. **Warm-up for lifting:** general warm-up value is unclear. Specific ramp-up sets are better supported.
4. **Push/pull ratio:** practitioner-only. Treat it as a soft rule.
